*** 181. Introduction: What is Transfer Learning and Why Use It ***
## **Lecture Summary: Transfer Learning**

### **What is Transfer Learning?**

* Transfer learning is the process of taking a machine learning model trained on one task and adapting it to a different, but related, task.
* Instead of building models from scratch, we reuse the learned patterns (weights/parameters) from an existing model trained on a large dataset.
* This is especially useful when you don’t have a massive dataset for your specific problem.

---

### **Example Use Case: Computer Vision**

* **Task**: Classify food images (e.g., Food Vision Mini).
* **Source Model**: A model trained on the **ImageNet** dataset (over 1 million images, 1,000 classes).
* **Approach**:

  * Use the patterns learned from ImageNet.
  * Customize (fine-tune) those patterns for the food classification task.
* **Benefit**: The model already knows general visual features (edges, textures, object shapes), which can be fine-tuned for more specific use cases.

---

### **Example Use Case: Natural Language Processing**

* **Task**: Build a spam detection model.
* **Source Model**: A language model trained on **Wikipedia**.
* **Approach**:

  * Use the pre-trained model’s understanding of language structure (syntax, semantics).
  * Customize it for email classification (e.g., spam vs. not spam).
* **Example**: In a sentence like *“The \_\_\_ jumped over the fence”*, the model might predict “dog” as a likely word due to learned context.

---

### **Key Concept**

* **Transfer Learning** = Pre-train a model on a large, general dataset → Fine-tune on a smaller, task-specific dataset.

---

### **Why Use Transfer Learning?**

#### **Main Benefits**

1. **Leverage Proven Architectures**

   * Use models that have been carefully designed and tested (e.g., EfficientNet, ResNet, Vision Transformers).
2. **Reuse Learned Patterns**

   * Instead of learning from scratch, start with pre-learned features that are often generalizable.
3. **Save Resources**

   * Requires **less data**, **less compute**, and **less time** to get good performance.
4. **Achieve Better Results**

   * Often leads to better model performance compared to training from scratch.

---

### **Research Support**

#### **From “How to Train Your ViT” (Vision Transformer)**

* **Key Quote**:

*** 182. Where Can You Find Pre-trained Models and What We Are Going to Cover ***
### **Lecture Summary: Transfer Learning & Pre-trained Models**

#### **1. What is Transfer Learning and Why Use It?**

* **Definition**: Transfer learning involves using pre-trained models on new tasks.
* **Benefits**:

  * Enables individuals with fewer resources and less data to perform world-class machine learning work.
  * Saves time and effort by leveraging existing knowledge.

---

#### **2. Sources for Pre-trained Models**

* **Main Resources**:

  * **PyTorch Domain Libraries**:

    * `torchvision` for vision models
    * `torchtext` for text models
    * `torchaudio` for audio models
    * `torchrec` for recommendation models
  * **Torch Image Models (TIMM)**:

    * A powerful library for computer vision models.
  * **Hugging Face Hub**:

    * Offers a wide range of models across modalities (vision, NLP, audio, etc.).
  * **Papers with Code (SOTA - State of the Art)**:

    * Source for the latest top-performing models and benchmarks in various ML domains.

---

#### **3. Deep Dive: TorchVision Models**

* **Accessing Pre-trained Models**:

  * Go to the PyTorch documentation, specifically `torchvision.models`.
  * Use models with pre-trained weights for tasks like image classification.
* **Model Examples**:

  * AlexNet, DenseNet, EfficientNet V2, MobileNet V3, Swin Transformer, Vision Transformer, etc.
* **Version Info**:

  * The lecture references `torchvision` version 0.12.
  * An unstable (future) release is being used for the demonstration (as of June 24, 2022).

---

#### **4. Why Use These Resources?**

* They provide access to top-tier models without needing to train from scratch.
* Ideal for experimenting and applying models to real-world problems efficiently.
* Great starting point for benchmarking and understanding what already works.
